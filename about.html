Our History

Kestrel Hill College was founded in 1889 by a small group of naturalists and teachers who wanted a school built around fieldwork rather than lecture halls alone. The original campus was a single stone hall, Aldergate Hall, still in use today as a first-year dormitory and the site of Lantern Night each October.

Over the following century, the college added the Observatory Library in 1932, the Founders Quad in 1958, and the Hollis Science Building in 1994, always keeping construction to the hill's natural terraces rather than clearing land for a flat campus grid.

Mission and Values

The mission of Kestrel Hill College is to teach students to observe carefully, argue rigorously, and work with their hands as well as their minds. That mission shows up in a required first-year fieldwork seminar, small seminar-style classes across every department, and a senior capstone project expected of all graduates.

Four values guide the college's decisions: close mentorship between students and faculty, interdisciplinary study that crosses department lines, small classes that make real discussion possible, and stewardship of the surrounding forest and river watershed the campus sits within.

The best classroom we have is the hillside itself.

— Founding charter of Kestrel Hill College, 1889

Leadership and Governance

Kestrel Hill College is led by President Marguerite Okafor, who joined the college in 2019 after a decade leading environmental research programs at a peer institution. She works alongside a provost, a dean of faculty, and a dean of students who oversee academic and campus life day to day.

A board of trustees made up of alumni, faculty, and members of the surrounding valley community meets quarterly to guide long-term planning, from capital projects to the college's endowment.

The Hillside Campus

The Kestrel Hill campus climbs the east face of Aldergate Hill in a series of six terraces connected by stone stairways and switchback paths. Buildings are arranged along the hill's natural contour lines rather than a flat grid, so nearly every classroom and dorm room has a view of the river valley below.

At the base of the hill sits Founders Quad, home to admissions and administrative offices. Partway up, the Observatory Library and Hollis Science Building sit side by side. Near the top, the upper-hill residence halls look out over the full Aldergate valley.
